# Research: Audemars Piguet Royal Oak and Gérald Genta

Compiled 2026-10-04. The main primary source is Audemars Piguet's own heritage site (AP Chronicles), which cites company archives. Note: AP is not a neutral party. It uses its archive to tell the story, but its numbers (sales, production) are the most specific we have. Where the watch press disagrees with AP, both versions are recorded below.

## Hook candidates
- **"A steel watch that cost more than gold."** In 1972 the steel Royal Oak was listed at CHF 3,300. AP's own 1972 catalogue had a yellow-gold model (ref. 5043) at CHF 2,990. That makes the steel watch dearer than AP's own gold watch, which is a safer comparison than the usual "more than a gold Patek". The most expensive steel watch before it sold for about CHF 850 (Time+Tide, citing AP's catalogue).
- **"Sketched in one night. Built in two years."** The overnight sketch (10–11 April 1970, per AP) is real according to AP and Genta. But the white-gold prototype only appeared in April 1971, the name only on 2 December 1971, and the launch was in April 1972. So the legend is true for the sketch, not for the watch.
- **"Nobody wanted it." Not quite.** AP's archive says 490 were sold in 1972, AP's best-selling single model since 1875. Other sources say the first batch of 1,000 took about three years to sell. Both can be true: a record for a small maison, but a slow sell-through. This tension makes a good slide.
- **A childhood memory on a Geneva bridge.** AP quotes Genta: the eight bolts came from a diver he saw being fitted with a helmet on Geneva's Pont de la Machine as a child. (Secondary sources say this comes from an interview recorded by AP shortly before his death in 2011. I did not confirm the interview date on AP's page.)
- **The name came last.** "Royal Oak" (after the Royal Navy ships, named in turn after the oak that hid Charles II) was proposed by Italian agent Carlo de Marchi and first appears in AP's archive on 2 December 1971, more than 18 months after the sketch.
- **Genta's own watch:** his personal steel 5402, the only one fitted with a yellow-gold bezel, sold at Sotheby's on 10 May 2022 for CHF 2,107,000. AP bid on it and dropped out at around CHF 1.7m.

## Disputed or myth — do NOT state as fact
- **Year of the overnight call: 1970 or 1971?** AP's archive and Monochrome say 10 April 1970, with the white-gold prototype shown "a full year" later at Basel in April 1971. SJX ("the afternoon before the Basel fair opened in 1971") and Swisswatches ("shortly before ... Basel in 1971") say 1971. AP's dated archive sequence is the most detailed and internally consistent version: sketch April 1970, prototype April 1971, case order 19 May 1971, name December 1971, launch April 1972. A 1971 sketch would leave almost no time before the May 1971 order for 1,000 cases. **Recommendation:** use "1970" and cite AP, or say "on the eve of the Basel Fair" without a year. Sources: https://apchronicles.audemarspiguet.com/en/article/birth-of-an-icon · https://watchesbysjx.com/2018/11/audemars-piguet-royal-oak-a-series-prototype.html · https://swisswatches-magazine.com/audemars-piguet-royal-oak-from-outcast-to-icon/
- **"Designed overnight."** The sketch was done overnight according to AP and Genta's own retelling, but there is no independent contemporary evidence beyond Genta and AP. Turning it into a watch took two years (prototype in 1971, name in December 1971, launch in April 1972). **Phrase it as:** "Genta said he sketched it overnight", or "the first sketch was drawn in one night". Do not say "the watch was designed overnight".
- **"The Italian distributor asked for it."** It was three SSIH agents (Italy, Switzerland, France), according to AP. Many retellings (e.g. Swisswatches: "a sporty steel watch for the Italian market") shorten this to the Italian market alone. De Marchi (Italy) did coin the name. Source: AP birth-of-an-icon.
- **Launch price: three values circulate.** CHF 3,300 (AP Chronicles, Time+Tide), CHF 3,500 (SJX), CHF 3,650 (Sotheby's, Swisswatches). **Use 3,300 and attribute it to AP**, or say "around CHF 3,300".
- **"More than a gold Patek" / "over ten times a Rolex Submariner."** Sotheby's states both, without a source. Monochrome puts the Submariner at about CHF 1,000–1,200, which makes the Royal Oak about 3x the price, not 10x. I could not find a primary 1972 Rolex or Patek price list. **Do NOT use "10x a Submariner".** The safe comparison is AP's own gold ref. 5043 at CHF 2,990 (Time+Tide citing AP's 1972 catalogue). Sources: https://www.sothebys.com/en/articles/50-years-of-an-icon-the-audemars-piguet-royal-oak · https://monochrome-watches.com/complete-history-of-the-audemars-piguet-royal-oak-jumbo-series-5402-14802-15002-15202-16202-review/
- **"Initial poor sales / a flop."** AP's archive says 490 were sold in 1972, a company record. Sotheby's says the first 1,000 took three years to sell. Swisswatches says "three years to sell 400 Royal Oaks in each country". AP's own later article admits it stayed under 7% of production until 1976 and that some saw it as a disappointment. **Fair framing:** "a record for AP, but slow to win over the trade and the wider market."
- **"Agnelli wore one in 1974 and that turned it around."** Swisswatches claims this. AP's archive (in the pages I read) does not mention it, and the AP data points instead to the 1976–77 new models (8638, 4100) as the real take-off. Treat it as an anecdote, not established cause.
- **"The Royal Oak saved AP from bankruptcy during the quartz crisis."** This is common in dealer and blog content. It is not supported by AP's own archive, which says the Royal Oak was under 7% of production and 6% of turnover from 1972 to 1976. The quartz shock is dated by AP from 1974, after the launch. **Do not claim it.** The Royal Oak can be framed as a bet on mechanical luxury made as quartz was emerging, not as a rescue. In 1970 the quartz crisis had barely begun (Seiko Astron, Dec 1969).
- **"6,050 made between 1972 and 1978."** One dealer page says this. AP says 6,050 is the total for the 5402 from 1972 to **2002**, across all metals. The error is useful as an example of how numbers drift.
- **"A-series = the first 1,000" vs "A-series = 2,000" vs "1,937".** SJX says the A-series is 2,000 (the first 1,000 plus another 1,000). AP says 1,937 steel A-series watches were made. Monochrome says 1,937 "sold between 1972–1989" (AP gives no end date for the A-series). Use "about 2,000 A-series numbers; AP counts 1,937 steel watches" if needed.
- **"The first luxury steel sports watch."** This is a widely repeated superlative (Wikipedia, Evelyne Genta via Quill & Pad, AP). It is defensible as "the watch that created the luxury steel sports category", but "first" is hard to prove. Use "widely regarded as".
- **Diving helmet: is the "eight bolts" detail true?** It is Genta's own memory, quoted by AP. Corselets with six, eight or twelve bolts did exist (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diving_helmet), so it is plausible. But a 3-, 6- or 12-bolt helmet photo would not match. Attribute it as "Genta said...". Some writers link the octagon to a ship's porthole instead. Genta used the porthole idea for the **Nautilus**, not the Royal Oak (https://www.geraldgenta-heritage.com/iconic-models).
- **"Bulova Royal Oak": that Genta designed it for Bulova first.** This is a forum myth. Bulova's 1970s lookalike was an homage. Source: https://www.woundforlife.com/2014/06/02/opinion-bulova-royal-oak-myth/ (blog, medium). Do not mention unless needed.
- **Nautilus "sketched in five minutes on a napkin in a restaurant".** This comes from the Genta Heritage site and Genta's own retelling. It is the same kind of legend as the Royal Oak sketch. Context only, and attribute it.
- **Name origin detail.** Some blogs say the ship hid Charles II. It was the **oak tree** (after Worcester, 1651). An unverified claim that the working name was "Safari" also circulates (Avi & Co., a dealer). Not confirmed by AP; omit.
- **Movement thickness.** AP says cal. 2121 is 3.05 mm. Fratello and others say "2.4/2.45 mm", which is the JLC 920 base. Use 3.05 mm if needed, or skip.

## Numbers to double-check
- Launch price: CHF 3,300 (AP) vs 3,500 (SJX) vs 3,650 (Sotheby's).
- AP gold ref. 5043 at CHF 2,990, and the previous priciest steel watch at about CHF 850 (only Time+Tide, citing AP's 1972 catalogue).
- 490 sold in 1972; first case order of 1,000 (19 May 1971).
- 5402 totals: 6,050 overall; 4,288 steel; A-series 1,937 (AP) vs "2,000" (SJX).
- Case 39 mm, about 7 mm thick (Monochrome says 7.15 mm, SJX/Fratello say "7 mm").
- The "Jumbo" nickname: retroactive, used after smaller sizes arrived (ref. 4100, 35 mm, 1977). The exact origin is not documented in what I read.
- Genta auction: CHF 2,107,000 including fees (Sotheby's, 10 May 2022).
- Rolex Submariner 1972 price: NOT verified (Monochrome says CHF 1,000–1,200).

## Summary for the editor
1. Strongest safe hook: the steel watch cost more than AP's own gold watch in 1972 (CHF 3,300 vs 2,990). Avoid "10x a Submariner" and "saved AP from quartz".
2. "Overnight" applies to the sketch (10–11 April 1970, per AP and Genta); the watch took two years (prototype 1971, name December 1971, launch April 1972). The year of the call (1970 vs 1971) and the price (3,300/3,500/3,650) are disputed.
3. Sales story: 490 in 1972 was a company record, yet under 7% of AP output until 1976. Best free image: OpaleHorse's 15202 (CC BY-SA 4.0). The Genta portrait is a company studio shot, so the editor should decide.
