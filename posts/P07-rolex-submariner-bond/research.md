# Research: Sean Connery, James Bond and the Rolex Submariner

Researched 2026-10-04. Note: rolex.com, watchtime.com, chrono24 and hodinkee pages could not be opened (403/blocked), so Rolex's own history claims are cited via secondary sources and flagged.

## Hook candidates
- **Bond's creator didn't own a Rolex when he gave Bond one.** Rolex shows up in Fleming's *Live and Let Die* (1954), but Fleming only bought his own Rolex (an Explorer 1016) somewhere between summer 1961 and spring 1962, around the time *Dr. No* was shooting in Jamaica. His heirs believe it was "the first and only Rolex he ever owned." (WatchTime, Feb 2009, Dell Deaton)
- **In 1958 Fleming said Bond *wouldn't* wear a Rolex.** A reader complained that Bond's watch stopped in *Doctor No* and suggested a Rolex Oyster Perpetual. Fleming replied that Bond found the Rolex too heavy ("about six ounces") for combat, and that Bond preferred "fairly cheap, expendable wrist watches on expanding metal bracelets" he could use as a knuckle-duster. (WatchTime 2009)
- **The world's most famous "NATO strap" isn't a NATO.** The striped strap in *Goldfinger* (1964) came nine years before the British MoD strap that got the "NATO" name (1973). NATO's own website says so. And for decades fans copied the wrong colours (black/grey). On Blu-ray it turns out to be navy, olive/green and a thin red stripe.
- **No one knows where the screen-worn watch went.** Even the story of how it got onto Connery's wrist has three competing versions.
- **A "James Bond Submariner" failed to sell at Sotheby's (2019)**, while Roger Moore's modified buzzsaw 5513 prop fetched CHF 365,000 at Phillips (2015).

## Disputed or myth: do NOT state as fact
- **"Cubby Broccoli took off his own Submariner and gave it to Connery."** This is the most repeated version (BeckerTime, Chronopedia, Perpetual Passion, Luxury Watches USA), but all of them hedge it with "legend has it" or "supposedly", and none cites a primary source. Other versions: director Terence Young lent his own; a Royal Navy diver on the crew lent his; it was Connery's own watch. (Those three alternatives appear only in search snippets; the pages could not be opened.) The safest line is: "How the Rolex reached Connery's wrist is disputed. The popular story credits producer Cubby Broccoli." Even WatchTime/Deaton writes "Whoever made that happen..." Sources: https://beckertime.com/blog/the-top-5-rolex-references-from-iconic-movies-the-rolex-submariner-ref-6538-from-dr-no/ ; https://chronopedia.club/The_Rolex_Submariner_and_James_Bond ; https://www.jamesbondwatches.com/refs/WatchTime2009Feb.pdf
- **"Rolex refused to lend watches / the budget couldn't afford one."** No sourced evidence. It's part of the same legend. Source: https://luxurywatchesusa.com/rolex-submariner-james-bond-reference-6538/ (unsourced)
- **"Goldfinger's NATO strap."** It is not a NATO. The NATO/G10 dates from 1973. Source: nato.int (above).
- **Strap colours.** Long believed to be black/grey. DVD/Blu-ray shows dark blue, dark green/olive and a thin red/burgundy stripe. Source: Bond Dossier; Everest Bands.
- **Strap width.** Sources disagree: 16 mm (several sites), 18 mm (Everest Bands), "20mm" (Bond Dossier). Gear Patrol garbles it. Everyone agrees it looks too narrow for the 20 mm lugs. Say "visibly too narrow" and give no number.
- **The strap came from a crew member at the last minute.** Plausible folklore, but not documented.
- **Dr. No strap colour.** Bob's Watches says "black crocodile leather". BeckerTime says brown leather. OmegaForums has a thread debating it. Say "leather strap" only.
- **Which 6538 dial (2-line vs 4-line) was on screen.** Rolex Passion Report says 2-line. Many sellers imply 4-line. Avoid.
- **"Same watch in all four films."** Sources say Connery wore "a 6538" in all four. That it was one and the same physical watch is not established. BeckerTime even implies a different 6538 in *Goldfinger*.
- **"Submariner = first dive watch."** Blancpain's Fifty Fathoms (1953) claims priority. Rolex's claim is "first divers' wristwatch waterproof to 100 m". Fifty Fathoms was rated 50 fathoms ≈ 91.4 m (Wikipedia says "94.5m", which looks like an error, since 50 fathoms = 91.44 m). Wikipedia is the only source for this. Don't call either "the first" without qualification.
- **"Rolex was Bond's watch in the books from the start."** Only partly true. One mention in 1954, then nothing for almost a decade, and in 1958 Fleming said Bond wore cheap watches. Source: WatchTime 2009.
- **Swisswatches Magazine says Dalton wore a 16610 in "The Living Daylights (1989)".** That's wrong: TLD is 1987 and LTK is 1989. Do not copy.
- **"A Connery-worn Submariner sold for $567,000 in 2018".** This appeared in an AI search summary with no traceable source. Treat as false or unverified.

## Numbers to double-check
- 6538 production years: "ca. 1955–1959" (Bob's). Wikipedia mentions a 40 mm case "in 1959", which conflicts with 37 mm. Use 37 mm or omit.
- Crown 8 mm vs 6 mm standard (Bob's only).
- 200 m rating "a year after launch": from Rolex via snippet and Wikipedia. Rolex page not opened.
- About 4 million Submariners made 1953–2020, "$46 billion" (Bloomberg Nov 2024 via Wikipedia, not opened).
- Phillips 2015 date: page header says Saturday 7 Nov 2015, other reports say 8 Nov. Use "November 2015".
- 2001 Christie's price $37,560 and 2011 CHF 219,000: single source (originalprop).
- Fleming letter: "about six ounces" (body text) vs "six ounces" (pull-quote) in the same article. Six ounces ≈ 170 g.
- Sotheby's 2019 estimate $180k–280k.

## Summary for the editor
1. Strongest hook: Fleming named Rolex in 1954, said in 1958 that Bond wore cheap watches, and only bought his own Rolex (an Explorer) around the time *Dr. No* was shot in 1962. This is documented by WatchTime 2009, which quotes the letters supplied by Fleming's stepdaughter.
2. Present the Broccoli "watch off his wrist" story as a legend, not as fact. Same for the strap width and the Dr. No strap colour. The *Goldfinger* "NATO" is safely debunkable via nato.int (the NATO strap dates from 1973).
3. No auction record exists for a Connery screen-worn Submariner. Use Moore's 5513 (CHF 365,000, Phillips 2015) and the 2019 Sotheby's "Bond" 6538 that didn't sell. Best free images: the EMore98 6538 (CC BY-SA 4.0) and the 1964 Comet Photo/ETH Connery photo (CC BY-SA 4.0).
