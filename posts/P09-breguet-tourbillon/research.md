# Research: Abraham-Louis Breguet and the invention of the tourbillon

## Hook candidates
- **"The tourbillon was built to fix a problem your wristwatch doesn't have."** It was patented in 1801 to correct errors in *pocket* watches, which sat upright in a waistcoat pocket for hours. A wristwatch moves through dozens of positions an hour anyway, so many horologists say the tourbillon adds little or nothing to accuracy on the wrist. Today it's mostly a showpiece. (Breguet patent page; Quill & Pad; Wikipedia/Tourbillon)
- **"In his whole lifetime Breguet sold only 35 tourbillons."** That's the firm's own figure for 1805–1823. About 40 were made between 1796 and 1829, plus 9 left unfinished or scrapped. (Breguet; Swatch Group archive; Revolution)
- **"The world's most famous watch was made for a queen who never saw it."** No. 160 "Marie-Antoinette" was ordered in 1783 with no limit on time or cost. It was finished in 1827, 34 years after she was guillotined and 4 years after Breguet himself died. (SJX; Quill & Pad)
- **"Breguet's 'first tourbillon' sits inside another man's watch."** The British Museum holds Arnold's chronometer No. 11 (1774), which Breguet fitted with a tourbillon in 1808. Its plaque reads "1er régulateur à tourbillon de Breguet réuni à un des premiers ouvrages d'Arnold… hommage… à la mémoire révérée d'Arnold, offert à son fils an 1808." It was a tribute to his late friend. (British Museum image record)
- **"The date on the patent isn't 1801. It's 7 Messidor, Year IX."** France was using the Republican calendar at the time. (Breguet; Revolution)
- **A fake Breguet in the Met.** The Metropolitan Museum owns a c.1810 "Breguet" watch made by an *imitator* in La Chaux-de-Fonds. Breguet put a near-invisible "secret signature" on his dials, engraved by machine, to fight counterfeits like this one. (Met/Commons CC0; secret signature: medium confidence, see below)
- Stolen in 1983, missing 23 years: No. 160 was taken from the L.A. Mayer Museum in Jerusalem by Na'aman Diller. It came back in 2006 through a lawyer acting for his widow. (Wikipedia/Diller; Aron Heller AP 2008)

## Disputed or myth: do NOT state as fact
- **"Breguet made No. 160 for Marie Antoinette / she owned it."** It was ordered *for* her by an anonymous Guard officer and finished in 1827. She was executed in 1793 and never saw it. Breguet: "identity unknown". · Breguet; SJX
- **"Axel von Fersen, her lover, ordered it."** This is a legend. Quill & Pad says Fersen is only "believed" to be the officer, and there is no firm evidence about the affair either. SJX says "according to legend". Say "legend says".
- **No. 160 completion date conflicts.** Wikipedia's "Marie Antoinette (watch)" and Breguet biography pages say c.**1802** (19 years). SJX, Quill & Pad (Elizabeth Doerr) and most watch press say **1827** (44 years, finished after Breguet's death). Use 1827 with the specialist sources, or hedge.
- **No. 160 cost:** 30,000 francs (Wikipedia) vs 17,000–30,000, with George Daniels documenting 16,864 francs (Quill & Pad). Avoid a single figure.
- **No. 160 recovery year:** 2006 (Wikipedia/Diller, Museum wiki, AP) vs 2007 (Breguet, SJX, Wikipedia watch article) vs "Nov 2008" (Quill & Pad, which actually confuses this with the date the thief was named). Wikipedia's watch article also says it was stolen on "17 April". Most sources say 15 April. Safest: "stolen in 1983, back in the museum more than 20 years later".
- **"Marat saved Breguet's life with a safe-conduct."** This is family tradition. Emmanuel Breguet (via WorldTempus) says it is "likely" he got the passport with Marat's help. The companion story, that Breguet once saved Marat by dressing him as an old woman, is explicitly "family tradition". Timing problem: Marat was assassinated on **13 July 1793**, and one source puts Breguet's departure on **12 August 1793**, so any help must have come earlier. At most say "reportedly with the help of Jean-Paul Marat". · https://en.worldtempus.com/article/industry-news/people-and-interviews/breguet-abraham-louis-breguet-the-man-behind-the-watchmaker-23933.html ; antiquarian-horology; Wikipedia
- **"He spent his exile in England / worked for George III."** Wikipedia and antiquarian-horology say so. Breguet and FHS describe exile in Switzerland only (Geneva, Neuchâtel, Le Locle, 1793–95). His London trips (1789–91 and other visits) are documented separately. Don't claim he lived in England during the exile.
- **"Breguet invented the wristwatch / the Queen of Naples watch was the first wristwatch."** The watch is **lost**. Earlier references exist: Elizabeth I's "arm watch" from Robert Dudley (1571) and a Jaquet-Droz & Leschot ledger entry (1790) for "a watch to be fixed to a bracelet". Safe wording: "one of the earliest documented watches made to be worn on the wrist". · search-level sources (watchexchange.sg, Swatch archive). The 1571/1790 claims came from search snippets only and weren't opened. Verify before using.
- **"The Queen of Naples watch was designed with John Arnold"** (Wikipedia Breguet article). Impossible: Arnold died on 11 Aug 1799. · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Arnold_(watchmaker)
- **"Arnold, not Breguet, invented the tourbillon."** Wikipedia (Tourbillon and Arnold pages) says Arnold "appears to have been the first to think of the concept". That's speculative. The patent and the working watches are Breguet's. Mention the friendship, not a priority dispute.
- **"The tourbillon makes a watch more accurate."** This is disputed even for pocket watches (Wikipedia: "debate… whether tourbillons ever improved the accuracy"). In wristwatches the consensus is that the benefit is negligible: the wrist averages positions on its own, and modern alloys and silicon hairsprings removed most of the problem. Counterpoints: Omega's 1947–52 tourbillon prototypes set observatory records, and 19th-century tourbillons and Bonniksen karrusels did well in trials. Present it as a debate. · Quill & Pad 2024/25; SJX Omega; Wikipedia
- **"Breguet came up with the tourbillon while in Swiss exile."** Swatch Group/Breguet and FHS say the idea likely came in 1793–95. That's a plausible narrative, not documented. Say "is said to have".
- **"Breguet's tourbillon turned once a minute."** That's what the patent describes. More than half of the watches actually made turn every 4 or 6 minutes.
- **"Fewer than a thousand tourbillons 1801–1945"** (Wikipedia). The source is unclear. Don't use.
- **Overcoil 1795, secret signature 1795 vs 1796:** brand and dealer sources, read at search level only. The secret signature is given as 1795 by one source and 1796 by another. Hedge ("in the mid-1790s").
- **Pare-chute "1790":** Breguet itself says testing started around 1790 and the definitive version came in 1806. The Talleyrand "watch thrown on the floor" anecdote comes only from the brand. Present it as an anecdote ("the story goes").

## Numbers to double-check
- 35 tourbillons sold 1805–1823; 40 made 1796–1829 (+9 unfinished); "nearly 30 survive" (Revolution only)
- 26 June 1801 = 7 Messidor an IX; patent lasted 10 years
- No. 160: 1783 → 1827 (44 years), 823 parts (Q&P says 823 refers to the 2008 replica; SJX says "over 820"). Number of complications varies by source (23 vs "a dozen"), so avoid it
- No. 160 value "$30 million" (estimate, Wikipedia/Q&P). Not an appraisal, so if used write "estimated"
- Brisbane No. 2574: 2,000 francs in 1816 → CHF 560,000 (2020)
- Napoleon: watch Nos. 38, 178, 216 (April 1798)
- Caroline Murat: 34 pieces 1808–1814; No. 2639 ordered 1810, delivered 1812
- Helwig flying tourbillon: 1920 (Wikipedia) vs 1927 for the model in the Glashütte museum (Commons caption). The model's date isn't necessarily the invention date
- Omega 30I: ~12 (maybe 13) prototypes; 867.7 points in 1950

## Summary for the editor
1. Facts safe to use: patent 26 June 1801 (7 Messidor an IX), 35 tourbillons sold in his lifetime, No. 160 ordered in 1783 with no limit on time or cost, Arnold No. 11 tribute (1808, British Museum), Napoleon's three purchases (1798).
2. Hedge or avoid: Marat's rescue (family tradition, and Marat died before the departure date), Fersen as the buyer (legend), "first wristwatch" (the watch is lost and earlier mentions exist), No. 160 completion 1802 vs 1827 and recovery 2006 vs 2007, and any claim that the tourbillon makes a wristwatch more accurate.
3. Best visuals: Breguet's 1801 patent drawing (PD), the c.1810 portrait (PD), the No. 160 photo in Jerusalem (CC BY-SA 4.0) and a modern tourbillon macro (CC BY-SA 2.0).
