Ian Fleming gave James Bond a Rolex in 1954. Four years later, he said Bond preferred cheap watches. 🍸⌚

Then Dr. No arrived in 1962, and Sean Connery's Submariner 6538 turned a tool watch into a legend. How it got onto his wrist is still disputed, and where it went is still unknown.

P08 · Audemars Piguet Royal Oak and Gérald Genta

Brand Stories · 2026-10-08 15:00 (Europe/Rome) · auto via GitHub Actions
Caption
In 1972, Audemars Piguet asked people to pay more for steel than for gold. 🔩

The Royal Oak was sketched by Gérald Genta in one night, inspired by a diving helmet, but it took two years to build and years more to win people over. Today it's one of the most famous watches ever designed.

Royal Oak or Nautilus? 👇

P09 · Breguet and the invention of the tourbillon

Horology History · 2026-10-11 15:00 (Europe/Rome) · auto via GitHub Actions
Caption
In 1801, Breguet invented a spinning cage to fight gravity. 225 years later, it's still watchmaking's ultimate flex. 🌀

The tourbillon was designed for pocket watches stuck upright in a waistcoat. Breguet sold only 35 in his lifetime, and experts still debate whether it helps a wristwatch at all.

Would you buy a tourbillon? 👇
