# Fact-check: Why watch ads show 10:10 (and why we show 10:04)
Verdict: PASS WITH FIXES

| # | Where | Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Slide 1 | Watch ads almost always show ~10:10 | ✅ VERIFIED | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clock_face ("customary for modern advertisements… approximately 10:10"); Karim et al. 2017 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5572348/ |
| 2 | Slide 2 | 10:10 forms a V and leaves the brand name under the 12 uncovered | ✅ VERIFIED | Wikipedia Clock face: "leaves the watch company's logo unobscured"; logo "usually appears in the upper part". Date window/subdials is a reasonable generalisation (varies by model). |
| 3 | Slide 2 / caption | A 2017 study found 10:10 made people feel happier and more likely to buy | ✅ VERIFIED (with nuance) | Karim et al. 2017, Frontiers in Psychology: 10:10 gave significantly higher pleasure and intention to buy vs 11:30. The study measured "pleasure", and the purchase effect was small. "Happier" is an acceptable paraphrase. |
| 4 | Slide 2 | Watches set to 8:20 ("frown") had no such effect | ✅ VERIFIED | Karim et al.: "Watches set at 8:20 did not show any effect on the emotion or the intention to buy." |
| 5 | Slide 4 | Early ads often used 8:20, which is also symmetrical | ✅ VERIFIED | Karim et al. (citing NYT, Newman 2008): "in the 1920s and 1930s, watches were almost always set at 8:20"; PetaPixel 2022: symmetrical and doesn't hide the logo. |
| 6 | Slide 4 | "Between the 1920s and the 1950s, brands switched to 10:10" | ⚠️ IMPRECISE | Sources say 8:20 dominated the 1920s and 30s and 10:10 became the norm by the 1950s. So the switch happened roughly 1940s–50s, not starting in the 1920s. |
| 7 | Slide 5 | A popular myth links 10:10 to the deaths of Lincoln or JFK | ✅ VERIFIED (it exists) | https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/22461/why-1010-default-setting-clocks-and-watches ; Snopes "The Ten-Ten Tenet" (listed in search, paywalled) |
| 8 | Slide 5 | Lincoln died at 7:22 a.m. | ✅ VERIFIED | Died about 7:22 a.m., 15 Apr 1865 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Abraham_Lincoln). Note: he was **shot** at about 10:15 p.m., which is close to 10:10 and is probably where the myth comes from. |
| 9 | Slide 5 | JFK was declared dead at 1:00 p.m. | ✅ VERIFIED | Pronounced dead 1:00 p.m. CST, 22 Nov 1963 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_John_F._Kennedy). Shot at 12:30 p.m. |
| 10 | Slide 6 | Lightning struck the Hill Valley clock tower at 10:04 on Nov 12, 1955 (BTTF) | ✅ VERIFIED | 10:04 p.m., Sat 12 Nov 1955 in *Back to the Future* (1985); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_Valley_(Back_to_the_Future) and the film's dialogue |
| 11 | Slide 6 | 10:04 is "the moment time travel became possible" | ❌ WRONG (in-universe) | In the film, Doc conceives time travel on 5 Nov 1955, and Marty has already travelled from 1985. The 10:04 strike is what powers Marty's **return** to 1985. |
| 12 | Caption | "Every watch ad shows 10:10" | ⚠️ OVERSTATED | Slide 1 correctly says "almost always". Some brands use other times, e.g. ~10:08–10:09 or 1:50 (Wikipedia). |
| 13 | Caption | 10:04 is when lightning struck the Hill Valley clock tower | ✅ VERIFIED | As #10 |

## Required fixes
- Slide 6: "**Our logo stops at the moment time travel became possible.**" → "**Our logo stops at the moment lightning sent Marty back to the future.**"
- Slide 4: "Between the 1920s and the 1950s, brands switched to 10:10, and it stuck." → "By the 1950s, brands had switched to 10:10, and it stuck."
- Caption: "Every watch ad shows 10:10." → "Almost every watch ad shows 10:10."
