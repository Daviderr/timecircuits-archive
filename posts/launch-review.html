L1 · Omega Speedmaster: from racetrack to the Moon

Icons & Curiosities · Launch #1 · manual post after warm-up
Caption
It started as a racing chronograph. It ended up on the Moon. 🌕

In 1964 NASA put chronographs from several brands through heat, cold, vacuum and shock. Only the Omega Speedmaster survived. Five years later, Buzz Aldrin wore one on the lunar surface, and that watch has been missing ever since.

L2 · John Harrison and the longitude problem

Horology History · Launch #2 · manual post after warm-up
Caption
Thousands of sailors died because nobody could fix a ship's position at sea. ⚓

In 1714 Britain offered a fortune to whoever solved the longitude problem. Everyone expected an astronomer. Instead, a self-taught carpenter spent decades building ever-better sea clocks, and his H4 changed navigation forever. Officially, the Longitude Prize was never awarded to anyone, though Harrison was eventually paid over £23,000.

Would you have backed the stars or the clock? 👇

Sources: Royal Museums Greenwich; Wikipedia (John Harrison; Scilly naval disaster of 1707).

L3 · Cartier Santos: the watch made for a man who flew

Brand Stories · Launch #3 · manual post after warm-up
Caption
Before 1904, a wristwatch on a gentleman was seen as women's jewelry. ✈️

Brazilian aviator Alberto Santos-Dumont needed to check the time without letting go of the controls. His friend Louis Cartier designed a square watch with a leather strap, and helped make the men's wristwatch a normal thing.

Fun fact for time travelers: his record-breaking flight happened on November 12. ⚡

Sources: Cartier; FAI; Quill & Pad; Wikipedia.

L4 · The Quartz Crisis

Horology History · Launch #4 · manual post after warm-up
Caption
In 1969 a watch with a battery nearly ended centuries of Swiss watchmaking. ⚡

The twist: the Swiss had working quartz prototypes too, and they beat Seiko's in 1967. But Seiko put one on sale first. Within 15 years, two out of three Swiss watch jobs were gone, until a plastic watch called Swatch changed everything.

Quartz or mechanical? Tell us 👇

L5 · Marty McFly's watch: what most fans get wrong

Most people say CA-53W, but in the 1985 original it's the CA-50. The CA-53W only appears in Part II and Part III. And in 2025, for the film's 40th anniversary, Casio released a special calculator watch inspired by the DeLorean.
